Part 2 Questions and Answers — Democratic Beliefs, Rights and Liberties

Part 2 covers Australia's democratic beliefs and the rights and liberties that go with them — and, just as importantly, the responsibilities that come attached to those rights.

Each question below shows the correct answer and an explanation.

Showing 25 of 50 questions in this part.

  1. 1. What kind of government does Australia have?

    • A. A parliamentary democracy
    • B. A dictatorship
    • C. An absolute monarchy
    • D. A one-party state

    Answer: A. Australia is a parliamentary democracy — the people elect representatives to Parliament to make laws and govern on their behalf.

  2. 2. What does 'freedom of speech' mean in Australia?

    • A. You can say anything without any limits
    • B. People can say and write what they think, and peacefully protest, within the law
    • C. Only citizens can speak in public
    • D. You can only speak English in public

    Answer: B. Freedom of speech means people can say and write what they think, and peacefully protest against government decisions, provided they do so within the law.

  3. 3. What does 'freedom of association' mean?

    • A. You can associate with anyone in secret
    • B. People can join or leave any legal group, including political parties, trade unions and cultural organisations
    • C. Only citizens can join groups
    • D. You can associate only with people who share your beliefs

    Answer: B. Freedom of association means people can join or leave any legal organisation, such as political parties, trade unions, religious groups or cultural associations.

  4. 4. What does 'freedom of religion' mean in Australia?

    • A. Everyone must follow the same religion
    • B. People are free to follow any religion, or no religion, provided they obey the law
    • C. You must belong to a Christian church
    • D. Religion is banned in public

    Answer: B. In Australia people are free to follow any religion they choose, or no religion. All religious practices must obey Australian law.

  5. 5. Is Australia's government secular?

    • A. No, it is a religious government
    • B. Yes — government and religion are separate
    • C. Only in some states
    • D. Only at election time

    Answer: B. Australia has a secular government — government and religion are separate. There is no official state religion.

  6. 6. Can religious laws override Australian laws?

    • A. Yes, always
    • B. No — Australian law is the law of the land; religious laws have no legal force
    • C. Yes, in some states
    • D. Only for certain religions

    Answer: B. Australian law takes precedence over any religious rules. Religious laws such as Sharia or Canon law have no legal status in Australia.

  7. 7. What does the 'rule of law' mean?

    • A. Only politicians must follow the law
    • B. Everyone, including those who govern, is bound by the law
    • C. Judges make all the laws
    • D. There is one law for the rich and one for the poor

    Answer: B. The rule of law means all Australians are equal under the law and no one is above the law — including politicians and police.

  8. 8. Are men and women equal before the law in Australia?

    • A. No, men have more legal rights
    • B. Yes, men and women have equal rights under the law
    • C. Only married women are equal
    • D. Only in some states

    Answer: B. Men and women have equal rights in Australia and are equal before the law.

  9. 9. What is the right to vote in Australia?

    • A. A privilege given only to some
    • B. A right and responsibility of Australian citizens 18 years and over
    • C. Only available every ten years
    • D. Optional for citizens

    Answer: B. Voting in federal and state or territory elections is both a right and a responsibility of Australian citizens aged 18 and over.

  10. 10. What is the 'secret ballot'?

    • A. Voting done in a private booth so no one knows how you voted
    • B. A vote taken only by senators
    • C. A code used by political parties
    • D. A ballot only known to the government

    Answer: A. In Australia elections use a secret ballot — no one can find out how you voted unless you choose to tell them.

  11. 11. What is a 'fair go'?

    • A. An unfair advantage
    • B. Equal opportunity, judged on effort and ability, not background
    • C. A free service from the government
    • D. A right to lose

    Answer: B. A 'fair go' means that in Australia what someone achieves in life should be a result of their hard work and talents, not their background.

  12. 12. What does 'presumption of innocence' mean?

    • A. Everyone is guilty until they prove innocence
    • B. A person accused of a crime is considered innocent until proven guilty in court
    • C. Only citizens are presumed innocent
    • D. Police decide guilt before trial

    Answer: B. In Australia, everyone accused of a crime is presumed innocent until proven guilty by a court.

  13. 13. What is the right to a fair trial?

    • A. The right to skip trial
    • B. The right to be heard by an independent court, with legal representation and a fair process
    • C. The right to choose your own judge
    • D. The right to have no trial at all

    Answer: B. Everyone is entitled to a fair and public hearing by an independent and impartial court.

  14. 14. Do Australians have the right to peaceful protest?

    • A. No, protest is banned
    • B. Yes, people can peacefully protest against government decisions
    • C. Only members of political parties can protest
    • D. Only in wartime

    Answer: B. Australians have the right to peacefully protest against government decisions and to campaign to change laws.

  15. 15. Can Australians criticise the government?

    • A. No, criticising the government is illegal
    • B. Yes — freedom of speech includes the right to criticise the government
    • C. Only newspapers can
    • D. Only members of parliament can

    Answer: B. Freedom of speech in Australia includes the right to criticise government policies and public officials.

  16. 16. What is 'mutual respect' in Australian society?

    • A. Only respecting people who share your beliefs
    • B. Treating people from all backgrounds and beliefs with dignity and courtesy
    • C. Respecting only religious people
    • D. Respecting only people you know

    Answer: B. Mutual respect means treating others with dignity and courtesy, and accepting differences in background, beliefs and culture.

  17. 17. What is 'tolerance' in Australian society?

    • A. Only accepting your own views
    • B. Accepting the right of others to hold different views and beliefs
    • C. Accepting any behaviour, legal or illegal
    • D. Ignoring people who are different

    Answer: B. Tolerance means accepting the right of other people to hold different views, and treating them with respect.

  18. 18. Is racial or religious hatred acceptable in Australia?

    • A. Yes, it is a form of free speech
    • B. No — inciting hatred against others is against Australian values and unlawful
    • C. Only in private
    • D. Only against non-citizens

    Answer: B. Encouraging hatred, discrimination or violence against others because of race or religion is unlawful and against Australian values.

  19. 19. When did the Australian Constitution come into force?

    • A. 26 January 1788
    • B. 1 January 1901
    • C. 1945
    • D. 1967

    Answer: B. The Australian Constitution came into force on 1 January 1901, when the Commonwealth of Australia was proclaimed.

  20. 20. How can the Australian Constitution be changed?

    • A. By the Prime Minister alone
    • B. By a referendum — a majority of voters overall and a majority in at least four states
    • C. By the High Court
    • D. By an act of the Senate

    Answer: B. The Constitution can only be changed by a referendum: a national majority of voters and a majority of voters in at least four of the six states must vote 'yes'.

  21. 21. Are all Australians free to follow any religion?

    • A. No, only Christianity is allowed
    • B. Yes — freedom of religion is a fundamental principle in Australia
    • C. Only citizens can
    • D. Only if the religion is officially registered

    Answer: B. People in Australia are free to follow any religion or no religion, provided they obey Australian law.

  22. 22. Should individuals impose their religious beliefs on others in Australia?

    • A. Yes, everyone should share the same religion
    • B. No — people are free to follow the beliefs of their choice
    • C. Only if it's a majority religion
    • D. Only in religious neighbourhoods

    Answer: B. In Australia, no one has the right to impose their religious beliefs on others. Everyone is free to follow, or not follow, a religion.

  23. 23. What is 'equality of opportunity' in Australia?

    • A. Everyone earns the same amount of money
    • B. Everyone has the same chance to succeed based on effort and ability
    • C. Only some people can succeed
    • D. Only citizens have opportunities

    Answer: B. Equality of opportunity means everyone should get a 'fair go' — the chance to succeed based on effort and talent, not on background or wealth.

  24. 24. Is violence acceptable to resolve disagreements in Australia?

    • A. Yes, if words fail
    • B. No — Australians resolve differences peacefully and lawfully
    • C. Yes, in family matters
    • D. Yes, if it is a small conflict

    Answer: B. Australian values reject violence. Disagreements must be resolved peacefully through discussion, mediation and lawful means.

  25. 25. What is 'compassion for those in need' in Australian values?

    • A. Helping only your family
    • B. Supporting people in the community who need help, through volunteering and services
    • C. Ignoring people in need
    • D. Only donating money to charity

    Answer: B. Compassion for those in need is a core Australian value, shown by helping people in the community through volunteering, mateship and public services.
